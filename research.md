# RESEARCH --- THE LAST FRONTIER

**Project:** Fictional NASA exoplanet exploration mission\
**Project type:** Conceptual landing-page / immersive storytelling
experience\
**Research lens:** Jobs To Be Done (JTBD)\
**Narrative inspiration:** *Interstellar*\
**Status:** Foundational hypothesis document --- not validated user
research

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Project purpose

Create an immersive landing-page experience for a fictional NASA mission
investigating a potentially habitable world beyond our solar system.

The website should do more than announce a planetary discovery. It
should help its audience connect emotionally with humanity's pursuit of
discovery, understand why the mission matters, and feel invited to
follow or participate in that larger ambition.

The experience takes inspiration from the emotional themes of
*Interstellar*: the scale of the unknown, the responsibility to protect
what matters, the courage to go beyond familiar limits, and the hope
that discovery can open up a future we cannot yet imagine.

**The website is not selling a planet. It is inviting people to imagine
a future beyond the one they know.**

## 2. Main purpose

The main purpose is to make the audience feel the possibility of another
world before asking them to understand the science behind it.

The experience should:

-   Create a sense of awe at the scale and mystery of the universe.
-   Spark curiosity about what may exist beyond our solar system.
-   Explain the functional purpose of the mission: investigating and
    evaluating a potentially habitable exoplanet.
-   Make the human effort and uncertainty of exploration tangible.
-   Build hope and optimism around humanity's capacity to discover.
-   Invite the audience to follow or join the mission in a clearly
    defined way.

The primary emotional outcome is **awe, followed by hope**.

Fear of Earth's destruction should not be the primary motivator. This is
an exploration story, not a dystopian escape story.

## 3. Audience and context

The intended audience is people encountering the fictional mission
through its landing page. They are not necessarily astronauts,
scientists, or applicants to a real space programme.

The experience should work for someone who:

-   Is curious about space, exploration, and the possibility of other
    worlds.
-   May not know the technical details of exoplanet research.
-   Wants to understand why this mission matters.
-   Can be moved by the scale of the journey and the human ambition
    behind it.
-   May want to follow the mission or feel connected to humanity's
    pursuit of discovery.

This audience description is a design assumption, not a finding from
interviews or quantitative research.

## 4. JTBD framework

### 4.1 Emotional job --- primary

> When humanity faces uncertainty about its future, I want to believe
> that there are still worlds worth discovering, so I can feel hope
> rather than helplessness about what lies ahead.

This is the central emotional job. The audience is looking for a reason
to feel possibility, not simply information about a distant planet.

**Desired emotional outcome:** Hope, supported by awe and curiosity.

### 4.2 Functional job

> Discover and evaluate potentially habitable worlds beyond our solar
> system.

For the audience-facing experience, this translates into helping
visitors understand:

-   What the mission is investigating.
-   Why the destination is scientifically interesting.
-   What makes a world potentially habitable.
-   What the expedition is intended to observe, measure, or evaluate.
-   What is known, what remains uncertain, and what the mission hopes to
    learn.

The fictional mission should distinguish between a planet being a
candidate for habitability and evidence that life exists. Do not imply
that life has been discovered unless the mission narrative explicitly
establishes that fact.

### 4.3 Social job

> Feel part of humanity's pursuit of something greater than ourselves.

The audience should feel connected to a shared human endeavour:
curiosity, exploration, scientific progress, and the desire to
understand our place in the universe.

This does not mean pretending every visitor is personally joining the
astronaut in space. The website should give them a credible way to
participate at the level the fictional mission supports, such as
following mission updates, exploring discoveries, or joining a mission
community.

### Emotional hierarchy

The intended emotional progression is:

**Awe → Curiosity → Courage → Hope**

-   **Awe:** The audience feels small in the face of the universe.
-   **Curiosity:** The audience wants to know what lies beyond what
    humanity knows.
-   **Courage:** The audience recognises the determination required to
    explore the unknown.
-   **Hope:** The audience leaves feeling that discovery can open up
    possibilities for humanity.

Hope is the emotional destination. Awe is the opening hook.

## 6. The central tension

The concept is built around a human tension:

**We know only one home, yet we are driven to understand what else may
exist beyond it.**

The experience should hold two ideas together:

1.  The universe is immense, unfamiliar, and potentially indifferent to
    us.
2.  Humanity can still explore it through curiosity, courage,
    cooperation, and scientific effort.

The audience should feel small in scale, but not powerless in spirit.

Avoid framing the mission as proof that humanity must abandon Earth. The
mission is about exploration and possibility, not declaring another
world a replacement for our home.

## 7. What the landing page needs to communicate

The landing page should answer four audience questions through its
narrative:

### A. Why should I care?

Connect the search for another world to human curiosity, our desire to
understand the universe, and the possibility of a larger future.

### B. What are we trying to discover?

Explain that the mission is investigating a potentially habitable
exoplanet. Use clear, accessible language and a small number of
meaningful scientific details.

### C. What does the journey involve?

Make the distance, uncertainty, and effort of exploration tangible
through the astronaut's journey and supporting mission information.

### D. What can I do next?

Offer a clear, honest next step: follow the mission, explore its
objectives, receive updates, or join a fictional mission community. The
CTA must match the actual interaction provided by the prototype.

## 8. Experience implications

The astronaut is the human anchor of the experience. The planet is the
destination; the astronaut makes the journey emotionally legible.

The astronaut should remain visibly present throughout the journey so
visitors can follow one continuous expedition rather than watch

## 9. Content principles

-   Lead with human meaning, then provide scientific context.
-   Use concise, emotionally resonant statements rather than long blocks
    of exposition.
-   Make scientific information understandable without oversimplifying
    it.
-   Separate confirmed fictional mission findings from hypotheses and
    unknowns.
-   Do not claim the planet contains life when the mission is only
    evaluating habitability.
-   Do not use Earth's destruction as the primary emotional hook.
-   Make the CTA an invitation to participate in discovery, not a
    generic conversion button.
-   Ensure the story makes sense even if visitors do not read every
    annotation.

## 10. Design principles derived from the JTBD

### Make the unknown feel immense

Use darkness, scale, distance, silence, and carefully controlled light
to evoke awe.

### Make the journey feel human

Keep the lone astronaut visible and consistent. Their movement should
give the audience a point of reference within enormous environments.

### Make curiosity drive the scroll

Reveal information progressively. Each chapter should create a question
that the next chapter helps answer.

### Make transitions meaningful

A wormhole or gravitational passage should represent crossing from the
familiar into the unknown, not simply provide a visual effect.

### Make discovery feel earned

Allow the planetary reveal to breathe. Avoid immediately covering the
destination with interface elements.

### End with possibility

The final scene should leave visitors with hope and curiosity, not fear
or a false promise that another planet will replace Earth.

## 12. Success criteria

The experience should be evaluated against both comprehension and
emotional response.

### Functional comprehension

After viewing the experience, visitors should be able to explain:

-   What the mission is investigating.
-   Why the destination is worth studying.
-   What the mission hopes to learn.
-   What remains unknown.

### Emotional response

Visitors should report or demonstrate:

-   A sense of awe about the scale of the universe.
-   Curiosity about the destination and the mission.
-   Hope about humanity's capacity to explore and discover.
-   A sense of connection to the larger human endeavour.

### Social and participation response

Visitors should understand how they can follow or participate in the
mission and whether they want to take that next step.

These are proposed evaluation criteria. No numerical targets or success
rates have been established.
